    How Taal teaches

    The beat first. Then three notes. Then a song together.

    1. 1

      Tell us what you love

      Paste a few YouTube links: songs the kids ask for, and one each from mum and dad. They tell us your family's taste. We never copy the songs.

    2. 2

      See your sound card

      Taal blends those tastes into one picture of your family's sound: its speed, its mood and its instruments.

    3. 3

      Get a song that fits

      A new song is written for your child's level and for the instrument on their lap. Every note is one they can reach.

    4. 4

      Play it together

      The song stops and waits for your child. You learn the part underneath. Same song, two players.

    Doing beats watching

    Studies of young children find the benefits of music come from making it, not from listening to it. So every Taal song hands over the turn.

    Small voices, small steps

    Children of three to five sing comfortably across about six notes. Level 1 uses the lowest three.

    Better with you

    Children keep playing when a parent plays too. Taal gives you a part, not a seat in the audience.

    Your instrument

    Send a photo of whatever you have at home.

    A rainbow xylophone, a small keyboard, a ukulele, or two steel pots and a spoon. Taal reads the photo and builds the lesson around it: the same bars, the same colours, in the same order.
