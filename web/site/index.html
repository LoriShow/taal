    Who's playing?
    Your instrument
    Screen keys. Tap the big coloured key when it lights up. Listen first; the pink "Your turn" part is yours.
    Lyre. Lay it flat with the long brass strings nearest you. Count from the longest: that is string 1. Only the coloured strings are used, and the one to pluck glows. Pluck with one finger, gently. A coloured sticker by each used peg helps little hands.
    Piano. Sit in front of the middle of the piano. Find middle C: the white key just left of the two black keys in the middle. Put your right thumb on it (finger 1) and rest your other fingers on the coloured keys, as the numbers show. Press the key that glows; the small number says which finger.
    Xylophone. Put it flat in front of you with the longest bar on the left: that one is C. Hold the stick loosely, like a spoon, and let it bounce off the middle of the bar. Find the bar with the same letter as the one that glows; most toy xylophones print the letter on each bar. If yours has no letters, count up from the longest bar: C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C.
    Ukulele. Hold it like a hug, neck pointing to your left, strings facing out. From the top (nearest your face) the strings are G, C, E, A, as on the screen. A letter in the first column means play that string open: just pluck it with your thumb, no fingers needed. A number means first press that string with one finger just behind that fret (the metal line on the neck), then pluck.

    This song needs a note your instrument doesn't have here, so it plays on the screen keys.

    On a computer, the number keys play the notes too.

    How Taal teaches

    The beat first. Then three notes. Then a song together.

    1. 1

      Tell us what you love

      Paste a few YouTube links: songs the kids ask for, and one each from mum and dad. They tell us your family's taste. We never copy the songs.

    2. 2

      See your sound card

      Taal blends those tastes into one picture of your family's sound: its speed, its mood and its instruments.

    3. 3

      Get a song that fits

      A new song is written for your child's level and for the instrument on their lap. Every note is one they can reach.

    4. 4

      Play it together

      The song stops and waits for your child. You learn the part underneath. Same song, two players.

    Doing beats watching

    Studies of young children find the benefits of music come from making it, not from listening to it. So every Taal song hands over the turn.

    Small voices, small steps

    Children of three to five sing comfortably across about six notes. Level 1 uses the lowest three.

    Better with you

    Children keep playing when a parent plays too. Taal gives you a part, not a seat in the audience.
